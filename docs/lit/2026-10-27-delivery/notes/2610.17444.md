- **Leaned-on references, looked up:**
  - Arghal et al. 2026 = arXiv 2602.08964 (ICML 2026), the source of the grid-world setup. It covers goal-directedness of an LLM agent in a 2D grid world, with behavioural and representational evaluation. Not coding.

**One-line verdict:** this is a grid-world mechanistic study with no tools and no code.
- It supports the C5 principle in two forms: information that is decodable or reportable is not acted on, and visible text in context steers the action.
- It has a C4-like design: paired interventions at the same reasoning prefix, read out as a one-step forced choice.
- It is absent on C1, C2 and C6, and touches C3 only as an injected action assertion.

## Claim

- Task-relevant state information is often represented (decodable from activations, reportable on request) more reliably than it is reflected in the action. Sources: Abstract, §6.
- Reasoning lowers the belief–action gap R while the decoded belief gets more diffuse (Table 1, all 3,194 steps):
  - R: 0.467 → 0.268
  - entropy H: 1.494 → 2.085
  - value of perfect state information I: 0.214 → 0.314
- Action preference sharpens gradually and stabilises at a median of 0.49 of the reasoning trace (§4.1).
- Reported belief corrections explain almost none of the action revisions: only 14 of 682 action changes (2.1%) coincide with any change in report correctness (§4.2, Table 6).
- Reasoning-text edits strongly redirect the action (§5.1, E):
  - A transplanted reasoning prefix from another decision gives π(a_B) 0.598–0.673 vs π(a_A) 0.129–0.165.
  - One injected sentence flips the forced action in 62.5–100% of cuts, depending on current confidence.
- Activation edits at a site where action decisiveness is highly decodable (readout ρ = 0.771) barely steer (§5.2, Fig. 4):
  - rank-1 steering reaches the intended action in 3.6% of cases, the same as a random direction (3.6%)
  - full 12-token window transplant reaches the intended action in 20.8%

## Method (enough to reproduce)

1. **Representation-based beliefs** (§2.2, A.1).
   - Probe input is the activations of the 3 final prompt tokens (pre-reasoning) or the 3 final reasoning tokens before action generation (post-reasoning).
   - Layers 7, 15 and 23. Each token is 2,880-dim, giving an 8,640-dim concatenated input, standardised on the train split.
   - Separate probes: an MLP for position (categorical over walkable cells), and linear classifiers for has_key and door_open.
   - State-disjoint 80/20 split: no state appears in both partitions.
   - The three component distributions are multiplied and renormalised over valid states to give q̂(s).
   - Not stated: which layer feeds Tables 1 and 4.
2. **Report-based beliefs** (§2.2, A.2, App. B).
   - Separate forward passes over a 38-question registry:
     - 30 categorical questions (yes/no/unknown, labelled A/B/C): has key; door open; wall in each direction; goal/key/door adjacent in each direction; whether each move would hit a wall, leave the key held, or leave the door open
     - 8 coordinate questions answered as JSON row/col
   - Parsing is exact-match; anything else is recorded as invalid.
   - Scores come from one of three sources:
     - empirical frequencies over repeated samples
     - API log-probs renormalised over A/B/C
     - local logits divided by 0.7, then softmax
   - Reasoning-time panel: 4 wall questions, has_key and door_open, plus the after-move questions for the action read out at that prefix.
3. **Values from one-step maximum-entropy IRL** (§2.3, A.3).
   - State features φ_p(s) are the mean activation over all occurrences of s (layer not stated).
   - Linear value g = θᵀφ.
   - Boltzmann policy over the values of the 4 deterministic successor states, β = 1.
   - Fit by NLL to the agent's own actions: Adam, 500 epochs, lr 1e-3, cosine annealing, gradient clip 1.0, L2 regularisation, source-state-disjoint split.
   - A source state is kept only if all 4 successors have features.
   - Separate pre and post models, each fit to its own condition's actions.
   - Control: planner-supervised labels, i.e. shortest-path optimal actions (A.4, C.2).
4. **Decomposition** (§2.4, Eqs. 2–5).
   - Per-state regret: ρ(s,a) = max_a' g(T(s,a')) − g(T(s,a)).
   - Expected local regret: Δ = E_q̂[ρ(s,a_t)] = R + I, where:
     - R = max_a' E_q̂[g(T(s,a'))] − E_q̂[g(T(s,a_t))] is the belief–action gap
     - I = E_q̂[max_a' g(T(s,a'))] − max_a' E_q̂[g(T(s,a'))] is the expected value of perfect state information
   - H is the Shannon entropy of q̂.
5. **Preference tracking during reasoning** (§4, D.1).
   - At most 80 prefixes per decision, typically 20 tokens apart, giving 98,310 prefixes.
   - At each prefix, a fixed answer-forcing suffix is appended and a softmax is taken over the 4 action logits, giving π_{t,k}.
   - Preferred action is the argmax. Commitment is the earliest prefix after which the argmax never changes (Eq. 16).
   - Textual commitment is the first concluding sentence that names the eventual action and is not followed by a different action. It is found in 753 of 1,276 decisions.
   - Sentence segmentation rules are given in D.1.
   - Sentence functions are labelled by gpt-oss-20b with a rubric adapted from Bogdan et al. 2025, checked against 152 author labels.
6. **Hidden-state clustering** (D.4).
   - Layer-15 mean-pooled sentence activations; 25,000 of 153,622 sampled.
   - PCA to 100 components (77.84% of variance), then ℓ2 normalisation.
   - MiniBatch K-means with K = 12.
   - Enrichment is tested against within-decision shuffled events, with FDR correction.
7. **Text interventions** (§5.1, E).
   - (a) **Whole-chain transplant:** the prompt of decision A plus a reasoning prefix from decision B, whose emitted action differs, cut at about 10/50/90%. 396 mismatched pairs plus 396 congruent controls, each read out as a forced action.
   - (b) **Single-sentence injection:**
     - 240 prefixes in 6 bins of c = π(eventual action), 40 per bin, at most 3 per decision.
     - One of three sentences is appended:
       - counter-evidence: begins "Wait, I should double-check this" and asserts that the current runner-up action is the correct move
       - neutral: names no action
       - supportive: asserts the current top action
     - 720 forced-action evaluations. The main analysis uses the 201 cases where the runner-up differs from the eventually emitted action.
8. **Activation interventions** (§5.2, F).
   - Site: the residual stream over the final ≤12 reasoning tokens before the answer-forcing suffix.
   - Readout: a nonlinear predictor of inverse normalised action entropy, with trajectory-grouped splits.
   - Edits tested:
     - rank-1 action-associated steering (2× residual RMS) vs a norm-matched random direction
     - low-rank donor transplant
     - full-window donor transplant
     - attention/MLP sub-block skipping
     - an appended instruction to disregard the mismatched reasoning

## Setting, data, models

- **Environment:** a text-rendered, fully observable 9×9 DoorKey grid with walls, a key, a locked door and a goal (§2.1, Fig. 1).
  - Deterministic transitions. A move into a wall or locked door leaves the agent in place. The key is taken on entering its cell.
  - One fixed layout: 39 walkable cells, 142 valid states, s = (x, y, has_key, door_open).
- **Each step:** the model sees the full grid with coordinate labels and its key status, and no history, so the interaction is Markovian. It reasons, then emits LEFT, RIGHT, UP or DOWN.
- **Model:** gpt-oss-20b only, open-weight. The paper does not state its size; its activations are 2,880-dim. Sampling temperature 0.7.
- **Data:**
  - §3: 200 trajectories, 3,194 decisions.
  - §4: 95 trajectories, 1,276 decisions, 2,370,850 reasoning tokens. Median 1,480 tokens per decision (IQR 642–2,605, max 9,733).
  - Report panel: 46 decisions from 31 trajectories, 7,084 prefixes, 42,504 reports (D.3).
  - Not stated: whether the 95 trajectories are a subset of the 200.
  - Not reported: task success, the step cap, or the per-step optimal-action rate for the 3,194 decisions. The only optimality figure is that 76.7% of evaluated prefixes prefer a planner-optimal action.
- **"Pre-reasoning":** a separate decision with an action forced directly from the environment prompt before any reasoning tokens (§3). So "pre vs post" compares a no-reasoning decision with a reasoning decision, not two time points of the same decision.
- **Absent:** tools, code, a context budget, partial observability.

## Metric (exact definitions)

- **Δ, R, I, H:** as in Method step 4. R is the headline belief–action gap: expected value lost by the emitted action relative to the best single action under the decoded belief, scored with the agent-supervised IRL values.
- **Probe accuracy:** on held-out, state-disjoint data.
  - top-1 exact match
  - position top-1 within Manhattan distance k
  - mean probability mass within distance k (Table 4)
- **Report accuracy:** correctness of the most probable parsed answer. Uncertainty is report entropy in bits.
- **c_{t,k} = π_{t,k}(a_t):** probability on the eventual action. Decisiveness is 1 − H(π)/log 4 (Eq. 17).
- **Commitment position:** k_com divided by trace length.
- **Intervention efficacy:** fraction of cases where the forced top action changes at all ("any") or becomes the injected or donor action ("intended").
- **Prospective monitoring:** AUPRC for predicting an optimality loss within the next 3 sentence boundaries.

## Results (including weak and negative)

**Availability of beliefs and values** (§3.1; Tables 2–4; C.1–C.3):

- **Probes get worse after reasoning (pre → post):**

  | Measure | Pre | Post |
  |---|---|---|
  | has_key | 100% | 89.5% |
  | door_open | 100% | 88.6% |
  | Position top-1 (chance 2.56%) | 34.8% | 13.1% |
  | Within Manhattan distance 1 | 95.5% | 60.8% |
  | Within Manhattan distance 2 | 99.0% | 83.5% |
  | Mass within distance 1 | 93.1% | 59.2% |
  | Mass within distance 2 | 97.2% | 81.5% |

- **Agent-supervised IRL is weak:** held-out action prediction is 29.2% pre and 45.2% post, against 25% chance. The pre-reasoning action distribution is more imbalanced (C.3).
  - Planner-supervised labels are "substantially more recoverable" on the main grid, but no number is given.
  - A separate four-room label control goes from 45.4% to 96.0% test accuracy, and optimal-set membership from 36.9% to 99.2% (C.2).
  - A β sweep leaves the result qualitatively unchanged; no numbers are given.
- **Reports on the 33-state failure set** (Table 2; 132 state–question pairs per family):

  | Family | Greedy | 10-sample modal |
  |---|---|---|
  | Walls | 93.9% | 97.0% |
  | Goal adjacency | 100% | 100% |
  | Door adjacency | 99.2% | 100% |
  | Key adjacency | 67.4% | 97.0% |

- **Reports vs probes on 15 matched states** (Table 3; LEFT/RIGHT/UP/DOWN):
  - reports: 100% in every direction
  - pre-reasoning probes: 73.3 / 86.7 / 100 / 80.0%
  - post-reasoning probes: 73.3 / 93.3 / 93.3 / 93.3%
- **Wall hits:** in all 3 wall-hit failures of a 13-decision failure slice, the model correctly reports that the chosen direction is blocked and still picks it (§3.1, B.1). n = 3.

**Belief–action consistency** (Table 1, pre → post):

| Subset | Δ | R | I | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All steps (3,194) | 0.681 → 0.582 | 0.467 → 0.268 | 0.214 → 0.314 | 1.494 → 2.085 |
| All probes correct (2,465) | 0.676 → 0.554 | 0.514 → 0.288 | 0.163 → 0.266 | 1.106 → 1.811 |
| Probe held-out states (762) | 0.700 → 0.670 | 0.316 → 0.199 | 0.384 → 0.472 | 2.788 → 3.020 |

- No CIs or tests are given for these differences.
- On held-out states Δ barely moves (0.700 → 0.670).
- Δ = R + I checks to within rounding.

**Decision formation** (§4.1; D.2; Figs. 3, 6, 7):

- **Sharpening:**
  - Probability on the eventual action rises from 0.35 (first tenth) to 0.92 (final quarter). Decisiveness rises from 0.34 to 0.91.
  - Same pattern before the first decisional action mention (35,379 prefixes): 0.35 → 0.92 and 0.33 → 0.89.
  - At the final prefix, the forced action equals the emitted action in every decision.
- **Prompt prior:** at the empty prefix, probability on the eventual action is 0.34 (uniform is 0.25). By eventual action: RIGHT 0.66, LEFT 0.37, UP and DOWN 0.14.
- **Commitment timing:**
  - median commitment at 0.49 of the trace
  - median post-commitment share 50.6% (median 524 tokens, mean 887)
  - commitment at 0.32 in the shortest quartile of traces vs 0.60 in the longest
  - 37.0% of decisions commit before the first decisional action mention
  - median first decisional mention at 0.30; c stays ≥ 0.8 from a median of 0.65
  - RIGHT commits earliest (median 0.39), DOWN latest (0.61)
- **Text lags the decision:** the forced preference stabilises before the textual commitment in 99.87% of the 753 decisions that have one, with a median lead of 523 tokens.
- **Switching:**
  - Switch hazard falls from 0.133 (first decile) to 0.019 (last decile).
  - 6.0 switches on average before commitment.
  - P(any later switch | c > 0.9) = 0.089.
  - The preferred action is planner-optimal in 76.7% of prefixes.
- **Sentence functions** (1,004 decisions, 141,377 sentences; before → after commitment):
  - world-reading 68% → 29%
  - solving 25% → 45%
  - concluding 1% → 12%
  - Label precision against author labels: concluding 0.91, solving 0.78, world-reading 0.73, meta-checking 0.32 (treated as exploratory).
  - Against a position placebo, the world-reading drop (−0.17, placebo [−0.14, −0.09], p = 0.01) and the solving rise (+0.12, placebo [+0.05, +0.10], p = 0.01) exceed placebo. Concluding does not (+0.02, p = 0.20).
- **Keyword counts after commitment:**
  - grid vocabulary 0.37×
  - coordinate pairs 1.41×
  - checking words 3.73×
  - explicit answer mentions: 29 before vs 1,751 after

**Beliefs around commitment** (§4.2; D.3; Tables 5–6):

- **Report entropy and accuracy** (45 decisions):
  - entropy 0.392 → 0.336 bits (−0.056 [−0.105, −0.009])
  - accuracy 82.8% → 83.0% (+0.2 [0.0, 0.6] pp)
- **Report changes at the 682 action-change events:**
  - any report-correctness change: 14 (2.1%)
  - optimal → suboptimal: 6 of 161 (3.7%)
  - suboptimal → optimal: 3 of 182 (1.6%)
  - mean change in error: 0.0005 [−0.0022, 0.0027]
  - In 66 of 161 optimal→suboptimal switches, all six reports stay correct.
- **Prospective monitoring:**
  - 5,283 prefixes with an optimal preferred action; 347 are followed by a loss within 3 sentences (base AUPRC 0.066)
  - behavioural baseline AUPRC 0.273; adding reports gives 0.334 (+0.061 [0.014, 0.123])
  - The next-sentence version is inconclusive.
- **Attention** (exploratory, layer 15): at action changes, attention to the newest sentence rises by 0.0838 pp per token ([0.0442, 0.1314], 67 pairs). For the grid, key-status statement and earlier reasoning, the intervals include 0.

**Hidden states** (§4.3; D.4; Tables 7–8):

- **At commitment:** cluster C10 appears at 14.0% of commitment sentences vs 5.9% of position-matched controls (+8.2 pp). Smaller excesses: C9 +3.2, C2 +2.5, C5 +1.6, C7 +1.0 pp.
- **At action changes** (4.59% of sentences contain one), enrichment with FDR q = 0.0024:

  | Cluster | Any change | Optimal → suboptimal | Suboptimal → optimal |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | C6 | 1.65× | 2.07× | 1.77× |
  | C10 | 1.64× | 1.48× | 1.59× |
  | C9 | 1.41× | 1.45× | 1.44× |
  | C5 | 1.33× | 1.26× | 1.35× |

- **Weak semantic tie:** NMI between clusters and semantic labels is 0.131. HDBSCAN labelled 77.1% of sentences as noise.

**Causal tests** (§5; E; F):

- **Whole-chain transplant:**
  - π(a_B) 0.598–0.673 vs π(a_A) 0.129–0.165 across cuts.
  - At the late cut, the forced top action matches the reasoning-chain action in 98.5% of congruent pairs vs 68.9% of mismatched pairs (mean probability 0.971 vs 0.673).
- **Single sentence** (201 cases), switch rate to the injected action by confidence c:

  | c bin | Switch rate |
  |---|---|
  | < 0.7 | 100% |
  | 0.7–0.85 | 92.5% |
  | 0.85–0.95 | 90.0% |
  | ≥ 0.95 | 62.5% |

  - Spearman ρ(susceptibility, c) = −0.421 (p = 5.0e−10).
  - c correlates with position (ρ = 0.538), and the effect declines with progress (ρ = −0.436). Controlling for position leaves partial ρ = −0.175 (p = 0.013).
  - Controls: a neutral sentence flips 9.6% overall; a supportive sentence raises the top-action probability from 0.757 to 0.989.
- **Activation site:** readout ρ = 0.771 ± 0.009, vs 0.644 for a length oracle and 0.371 for a positional baseline.
- **Activation edits:**
  - rank-1 steering: 3.6% intended, equal to the random direction's 3.6%
  - low-rank transplant: 0 intended flips
  - full 12-token window transplant: 25.0% any change, 20.8% intended (best layer)
  - sub-block deletion: up to 29.2% any change (no intended target)
  - instruction to disregard the mismatched reasoning: 5.8% any change, 1.0% intended
  - So an explicit "ignore that text" barely undoes the influence of the visible transplanted reasoning.

**Negative or weak results, collected:**
- decoding is worse after reasoning
- IRL fits are weak
- report accuracy is flat around commitment
- activation edits do no better than random
- the next-sentence monitor is inconclusive
- the concluding-sentence shift is not beyond placebo

## Limitations

**The authors' own** (§6, B.1, D.3, F.2):
- a controlled deterministic grid world
- results are tied to these estimators and interventions
- probes show decodability, not the model's internal belief
- one-step IRL gives an inferred preference, not the true value function
- negative activation results hold only for the tested layers, subspaces and edit families, and there is no positive-control steering direction
- reports come from a separate forward pass, so they may recover information unused in the decision
- the attention result is observational

**Mine:**
1. **R rests on a weak value model.** It is defined against IRL values that predict only 29.2% (pre) and 45.2% (post) of held-out actions, against 25% chance. The pre and post models are fit separately to different action sets. A better post fit shrinks R by construction, so part of the R drop may be fit quality rather than better consistency. No matched-fit control is run.
2. **Sampling noise is in R.** Emitted actions are sampled at T = 0.7, so part of R is sampling noise, and there is no T = 0 comparison.
3. **The rise in H and I may be a readout artifact.** Post-reasoning probes read the last 3 reasoning tokens, which carry less grid information than the end of the prompt. The higher H and I after reasoning may reflect where the probe reads, not a more diffuse belief.
4. **Thin replication.** No CIs or tests for Table 1; one model; one layout; no seed replication; layers for Tables 1 and 4 and for the IRL features not stated.
5. **Small n for the key evidence.** The "reports correctly, acts wrongly" evidence is 3 wall-hit cases, and the report-vs-probe comparison is 15 states.
6. **Injected actions are not checked for wrongness.** The runner-up action injected in E.2 is not checked against the planner, so "switch" means redirection, not necessarily an error.
7. **One-step readouts only.** Every causal readout is a forced-choice logit after an answer-forcing suffix. There are no free continuations and no environment outcomes.
8. **The Fig. 8 early-cut anomaly** is not discussed.
9. **Self-labelling.** Sentence labels come from the model under study, and one category has precision 0.32.

## Assumptions the paper does not test

- That forced-answer logits at a prefix track the free-running decision. This is only validated at the final prefix.
- That a factorised, renormalised probe distribution can stand in for "the agent's belief".
- That the agent chooses as a Boltzmann policy over one fixed state-value function.
- That transplanted reasoning acts through decision dynamics rather than through the state description it carries (see Fig. 8).
- That the results carry over to tool use, partial observability, long histories, or other models.

## C5 focus: how the gap between belief and action is measured

Environment: grid world only, no coding. Model: gpt-oss-20b only.

1. **Belief–action gap R.** Expected value the emitted action loses against the best single action under the probe belief and the IRL values: 0.467 → 0.268 over all steps (Table 1).
2. **Stated belief vs action.**
   - 3 of 3 wall hits had a correct "blocked" report.
   - In 66 of 161 optimal→suboptimal switches, all six reports were correct.
   - Only 2.1% of 682 action changes coincide with any change in report correctness (Table 6).
3. **Decodable vs controllable.** Decisiveness is decodable at ρ = 0.771, but rank-1 steering is no better than random (3.6%) (Fig. 4).
4. **Visible text vs the prompt.**
   - Reasoning text in context outweighs the prompt grid: π(a_B) 0.598–0.673.
   - One asserted sentence flips the action in 62.5–100% of cases.
   - An instruction to ignore the transplanted reasoning restores the prompt's action in only 1.0% of cases (F.2).
5. **Text lags the decision.** The forced preference stabilises before the textual commitment in 99.87% of cases, a median of 523 tokens earlier. A monitor reading the reasoning text sees the decision late.

## What it does NOT do that the idea needs

- Any coding task, tools, code graph or end-to-end repair, and any context budget or quantised model.
- Pulled vs pushed delivery of the same information, or presentation variants (bare names vs signature + file:line).
- A planted false structural relation, scored against how often the agent visits the same target when the graph is true.
- Multi-step continuations from a saved state with shared seeds, or measuring where the agent reads or edits next. No paired tests such as McNemar.
- Counts of interface failures.
- More than one model.

**Worth borrowing:**
- **Base-rate triplet.** The counter / neutral / supportive design at the same prefix gives a clean base rate for C3.
- **Commitment as a covariate.** Susceptibility fell from 100% to 62.5% as confidence rose, so pre-register the agent's commitment at the fork point as a covariate for decoy-following.
- **Cheap secondary readout.** A forced-choice readout over candidate next targets at the fork could run next to the short continuations.
