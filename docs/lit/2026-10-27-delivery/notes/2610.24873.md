# The One-Pixel of Reasoning: One Truthful Relation Is Enough to Mislead LLM Agents

- **arXiv:** 2610.24873 (v1, 17 Oct 2026, cs.AI, CC BY 4.0). Checked with `lit.py verify` (title match 1.0, authors OK).
- **Authors:** Jinwei Hu, Youcheng Sun, Yi Dong, Xiaowei Huang (University of Liverpool; MBZUAI).
- **Version read:** v1. The arXiv HTML full text (3002 lines) and the 33-page PDF, read on 2026-10-27.
- **Leaned-on references, looked up:** Generative Montage, "Lying with Truths" (arXiv:2601.01685, same group), which is the nearest prior work it names. The First Drop of Ink (arXiv:2605.10828), on distractors in long contexts.
- **One-line verdict:** This is a single-turn attack on classifiers: yes/no/maybe medical QA, and vulnerable/safe on one C function. One added sentence flips the verdict. The sentence is true and source-grounded, and it qualifies one implicit evidence-to-conclusion "warrant". In spirit it is the nearest "one planted relation misleads" result to our idea. But it has no agent loop, no tools, no code graph, no pull-vs-push contrast and no forks of an agent state. It partly covers C3, C4 and C5, and does not cover C1, C2 or C6.

## Claim

- **Headline.** One sentence that "undercuts" a single warrant overturns answers the victim first got right. An undercutter qualifies the warrant's applicability or sufficiency without stating the wrong answer.
  - Best-of-10 ASR averages **54.9%** for 8 proprietary victims and **80.0%** for 10 open-weight victims, averaged over both domains (Abstract; Sec. 4.1; Table 1).
  - Re-derived from the Table 1 rows: 54.86 and 79.96.
- **Transfer without victim feedback.** Zero-query ASR ranges from 20.5% (GPT-5.6-terra, Cyber) to 83.1% (Qwen2.5-32B, Cyber) (Table 2).
- **Where to aim and what to add matter.** This comes from a paired 3-arm ablation (Sec. 4.4; Tables 5 and 12; exact McNemar in Table 15).
  - Setup: Luna is both attacker and victim, 100 items per domain, best-of-3.
  - Combined ASR: ORA 46.0%, random warrant 24.0%, random content 19.5%.
- **Attacks survive LLM audits** (Table 3; Table 14).
  - Joint (supported AND not exactly identified): 83.1% of medical query successes and 90.9% of Cyber query successes.
  - Those joint successes are 364/735 = 49.5% and 370/707 = 52.3% of Luna's clean-correct items (App. Q.2).
- **Debate makes it worse** (Table 4; Table 13; Fig. 3).
  - A 3-agent debate on the attacked evidence raises team error from 50.0% to 59.5% in a balanced stress test.
  - Contamination is 23.7% and recovery is 4.7%.
- **Theory** (Sec. 2, Proposition 1; proof in App. Q.1).
  - In an additive warrant-score model, attenuating one relation reverses the decision iff C_w = η_w · max_y [d_yw]₊ / γ_y > 1.
  - It also gives a k-relation extension and nonlinear (Lipschitz) bounds.

## Method (enough to reproduce)

ORA has five stages (Sec. 3; Fig. 1; Algorithm 1 in App. H; prompts in App. K).

1. **Argument-graph construction.** The attacker LLM, gpt-5.6-luna ("Luna"), extracts a set of warrants. Each warrant w maps grounds g(w) (a set of evidence units) to a claim c(w). For each warrant it records:
   - backing strength: strong, moderate or weak;
   - visible defeaters Def(w);
   - an answer-bearing flag a(w).

   In the medical domain the warrants are linked to PICO variables. In Cyber they cover operations, guards and data flow; every fact must occur in the function, and the verdict is vulnerable or safe.
2. **Critical-warrant localization** (App. G).
   - Score: RS(w) = L(w)·[λ·U_inst + (1−λ)·D(w)].
   - L = 1 for an answer-bearing warrant, and 0.4 otherwise.
   - D(w) = ½·min(1, κ_def·|Def(w)|) + ½·b(w), with b = 1, ½ or 0 for weak, moderate or strong backing.
   - κ_def = 0.34 (medical) and 0.5 (Cyber); λ = 0.5.
   - U_inst is the normalized entropy of the attacker's own answers. It uses 1 sample in medical, so it is 0 there, and 3 samples in Cyber.
3. **Undercutter generation.** The attacker writes up to K candidates, given the selected warrant w*, its grounds, the full source and the gold label.
   - The medical prompt asks for one TRUE sentence that is "non-probative" but "suggestive": a confounder, a small sample, association rather than causation, or an alternative explanation. It must not state a false result or the wrong answer.
   - Each candidate rotates a "diversity dimension":
     - medical: imprecision, indirectness, inconsistency, confounding, association vs causation;
     - Cyber: bounds, guards, sizes, inputs, pointer validity, control flow.
   - Generic call sites request temperature 0.9. The GPT-5 wrapper omits temperature (App. J).
4. **Lexical validation** (Eq. 7, App. A).
   - Medical: flags proper-name-like tokens and numbers that are absent from the source.
   - Cyber: flags word tokens and integers absent from the function.
   - Query mode ignores the flags; the zero-query fallback uses them.
5. **Release.**
   - Query mode tries the candidates in generation order against the victim. Each call contains the original evidence plus one sentence. It releases the first candidate that flips the answer, with at most K = 10 victim calls.
   - Zero-query mode: the attacker scores each candidate on grounded, stealthy and misleading (each in [0,1]) and picks one, with no victim feedback.

**How the sentence is inserted** (App. I, K).
- PubMedQA: as a new numbered evidence unit right after the selected grounding sentence. If that fails it goes at the end of the results section, and otherwise at the end of the abstract.
- PrimeVul: the function is untouched, and the sentence appears in a numbered "analyst note" block. **The clean branch has no note block at all.**

**Controls** (App. M).
- 100 eligible clean-correct items per domain (seed 20260924).
- Eligibility needs an answer-bearing top-saliency warrant plus at least one non-answer-bearing warrant whose grounds do not overlap.
- *Random warrant:* the same template, aimed at a non-answer-bearing warrant.
- *Random content:* restate one concrete detail of the critical grounds. It may not question sufficiency, add a caveat, recommend an answer or weaken the relation.
- 3 candidates per arm, with Luna as attacker and victim.

**Debate** (App. O, K).
- The team is Luna, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B and Phi-4.
- An item is eligible when all three are correct on the clean input and Luna's frozen best-of-10 sentence flips Luna.
- Procedure:
  - all agents see the attacked evidence;
  - Round 0 is independent;
  - there is one revision round, in which each agent sees the three anonymized Round-0 answers;
  - the team decision is a majority vote. Medical ties go to the larger summed confidence, then yes > no > maybe.
- Cohorts (seed 42):
  - medical: 149 correct and 149 wrong teams;
  - Cyber: 83 unanimous-wrong teams and 83 randomly sampled correct teams.

**Audits** (App. N, K). Luna serves both as support judge and as blind detector.
- The support judge counts explicit facts and "reasonable interpretations" as supported.
- The blind detector returns one suspicious unit id or "none", with no attack warning.

## Setting, data, models

**Data** (Sec. 4; App. I, J).
- 1000 PubMedQA items: the question, the full labeled abstract and a yes/no/maybe answer.
- 1000 PrimeVul functions: 500 vulnerable and 500 benign (seed 42), pooled from held-out validation and test. They are deduplicated, functions over 2500 characters are dropped, and nothing is truncated.

**Models** (App. I).
- Attacker: gpt-5.6-luna.
- 18 victims:
  - GPT-4.1 nano, mini and full;
  - GPT-5.4 nano, mini and full;
  - GPT-5.6 luna and terra;
  - DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 7B, 14B and 32B;
  - Qwen2.5-Instruct 7B, 14B and 32B;
  - Phi-4-mini-instruct, Phi-4, Phi-4-mini-reasoning and Phi-4-reasoning.

**Victim call.** One prompt returns JSON with a decision, a confidence in [0,1], a one-sentence reason, and "the ids of the sentences you relied on" (App. J, K). There is no tool use and no multi-step loop.

**Decoding** (App. J). It is not uniform across models.
- Target and graph calls request temperature 0.
- The GPT-5 wrapper omits temperature, uses low reasoning effort and allows at least 2048 output tokens.
- The vLLM wrapper has a temperature floor (default 0.6), top-p 0.95 and repetition penalty 1.15.
- 32B models: vLLM 0.6.6.post1, 2-way tensor parallel, 8192-token context, temperature 0.6.
- Phi reasoning models: FP16 with no quantization, up to 6144 output tokens within an 8192-token context.

## Metrics (exact definitions)

- **Belief leap** (Eq. 2): BL = 1[clean answer correct] · 1[attacked answer wrong].
- **ASR_v(K)** (Eq. 11, App. B): among completed clean-correct items, the share for which some candidate j ≤ K gives f_v(q, E ⊕ δ_j) ≠ y\*.
  - Missing attacks drop out of the denominator; they are not counted as successes.
  - In zero-query mode, ASR is the indicator for the single selected sentence.
- **Signed belief** B = (2·1[correct] − 1)·p.
  - Conf is the mean p among flips.
  - HC-Conf is the share of flips with p ≥ 0.8 (Eqs. 12–15).
  - **Response selection differs by domain.** Medical uses the *minimum* signed belief over all candidates, i.e. the strongest error recorded. Cyber uses the *first* successful candidate (App. B).
- **Audit metrics** (Eqs. 15–17).
  - FFR = 1 − judged Support.
  - Exact = the detector returns exactly the injected id.
  - Clean FP = any flag raised on a clean input.
  - Joint = supported AND not exactly identified.
- **Debate** (Eqs. 18, 29).
  - Contam = Pr(wrong after | correct before).
  - Recover = Pr(correct after | wrong before).
  - Final error ε′ = ε + (1−ε)c − εr.
- **Statistics.**
  - Exact two-sided McNemar on discordant pairs (Eq. 39).
  - A stratified hypergeometric test for the debate (Eqs. 40–41).
  - Wilson intervals (Eq. 42).
  - Belief bands come from 800 instance-bootstrap resamples (seed 0).

## Results, including weak and negative ones

**Table 1 averages** (re-derived; Acc / ASR):

| Group | Medical | Cyber |
|---|---|---|
| Proprietary | 71.4 / 57.8 | 64.0 / 51.9 |
| Open-weight | 64.9 / 66.8 | 54.4 / 93.1 |

**Cyber baselines are near chance** for the open-weight victims.
- Clean accuracy ranges from 50.2 (Qwen2.5-7B) to 59.5 (Phi-4), on a balanced binary task (Table 1).
- Each "initially correct" set is dominated by the model's default label (Table 11):
  - Qwen2.5-7B is right on 31 vulnerable and 471 benign functions;
  - DeepSeek-R1-7B is right on 406 vulnerable and 125 benign.
- So the 93.1% open-weight Cyber ASR mostly measures how easily a biased classifier's default label can be flipped. It is weak evidence that a reasoned verdict was overturned.

**Scale** (Sec. 4.1; Table 1).
- At 32B, medical accuracy rises over 14B.
- But medical ASR stays flat for Qwen2.5 (49.8 → 48.7) and rises for DeepSeek-R1 (70.6 → 76.7).
- Both 32B models exceed 97% ASR in Cyber.

**Reasoning-tuned variants** (App. D.1).
- Medical ASR is higher in all five comparisons:
  - DeepSeek-R1 minus Qwen2.5: +25.3, +20.8 and +28.0 points at 7B, 14B and 32B;
  - Phi-4-mini: 58.2 → 80.5, while accuracy rises 57.9 → 66.3;
  - Phi-4: 58.3 → 63.4.
- Cyber goes the other way for Phi: the reasoning variants are lower (91.7 vs 99.4, and 80.4 vs 89.1).
- These are different checkpoints, not a reasoning mode switched on and off in one model. The authors say so.

**Budget** (Table 10). Mean ASR at K = 1 versus K = 10:

| Group | K = 1 | K = 10 |
|---|---|---|
| Medical, proprietary | 18.6 | 57.8 |
| Medical, open-weight | 25.8 | 66.8 |
| Cyber, proprietary | 24.1 | 51.9 |
| Cyber, open-weight | 60.4 | 93.1 |

- Candidates 6 to 10 still add 7.5–10.3 points.
- **The headline number is a search rate.** The first candidate alone succeeds 18.6–60.4% of the time.

**Zero-query vs query** (Table 2).
- In medical, zero-query is roughly half of query (e.g. Phi-4-mini-R 80.5 → 40.1).
- In Cyber it is also lower (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-7B 92.5 → 46.9).

**Ablation** (Tables 5, 12, 15; App. M, R). Paired cells are listed as (ORA-only, control-only, both, neither).

| Domain | ORA | Random warrant | Random content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical, single candidate | 35.7 | 15.3 | 4.7 |
| Medical, best-of-3 | 55 | 27 | 11 |
| Cyber, single candidate | 20.0 | 11.0 | 17.0 |
| Cyber, best-of-3 | 37 | 21 | 28 |

| Comparison | Paired cells | p |
|---|---|---|
| ORA vs random warrant, medical | (31, 3, 24, 42) | 7.66e-7 |

  - In Cyber, a neutral restatement of grounds already present, delivered as an analyst note, flips 17% of single candidates and 28% at best-of-3.

**Audits** (Tables 3, 14; App. N).

| Setting | FFR | Exact | Clean FP | Joint [Wilson 95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medical, query | 11.9 | 7.5 | 25.6 | 83.1 [79.3, 86.3] |
| Medical, zero-query | 8.4 | 6.1 | 24.2 | 87.7 |

- Audit populations:
  - query audits cover Luna's 438 medical and 407 Cyber successes;
  - zero-query audits cover 999 and 1000 deployed sentences.
- The medical detector flags about a quarter of clean inputs, yet rarely finds the injected unit.

**Confidence.**
- Luna's medical flips have HC-Conf 100%, while GPT-5.4-nano's are 9.8% (medical) and 2.5% (Cyber) (Table 8).
- Luna's 100% uses the medical min-over-candidates selection rule, which picks the most confident error.
- In the full 32B Cyber cohorts, high-confidence errors make up 418/553 = 75.6% (DeepSeek-R1-32B) and 390/580 = 67.2% (Qwen2.5-32B) (App. B).
- Among flips, the mean paired belief shift is −1.65 (medical) and −1.74 (Cyber) (Fig. 4).

**Direction** (Table 11).
- At 32B, most recognized vulnerabilities flip to "safe": 290/293 for DeepSeek-R1 and 149/152 for Qwen2.5.
- Benign-to-vulnerable rates for those two models are .996 and .970.
- GPT-5.4 is the least movable: vulnerable-to-safe .379, benign-to-vulnerable .356.

**Debate** (Table 13).
- Of 232 teams correct before debate, 55 end up wrong. Of 232 wrong teams, 11 recover.
- Final error is 276/464 = 59.5%.
- Wilson intervals: contamination [18.7, 29.6], recovery [2.7, 8.3].
- The stratified exact p is 3.07e-9 (Table 15). The authors call these values exploratory and unadjusted (App. R).

**Case study** (App. C).
- Function do_viewlog, CWE-79 (HTML/script injection).
- The note says "buf[n] = 0 remains within the buffer". It moves Luna from +0.93 to −0.98 signed belief.
- The support judge accepts the note, and the detector flags nothing.

## Limitations

### The authors'

- The evidence pools are fixed, and the original evidence is always present. Real-world frequency depends on what an attacker can insert (Limitations).
- Only one attacker family is used. The evidence is static and in English only. End-to-end latency and cost in full agent pipelines are not measured. Defenses are left to future work (Limitations; App. T).
- The support judge comes from the attacker's own model family (App. P).
- The debate has no no-communication condition, so the effect of peer messages cannot be isolated (App. P). The debate p-values are exploratory (App. R).
- The reasoning comparisons are across different checkpoints (App. D.1), and decoding is not uniform (App. J).

### Ours

1. **It is not an agent.** Each victim call is one prompt and one JSON answer: no tools, no trajectory, no environment. "Agent" in the title is nominal for these experiments.
2. **There is no baseline from re-asking without the injection.** Victims decode stochastically (the vLLM temperature floor is 0.6, and GPT-5 omits temperature), and ASR is best-of-10 over fresh victim calls. The paper never reports how often simply re-asking the clean input up to 10 times flips a correct answer. The matched controls exist only for Luna, on 100 items per domain, at best-of-3.
3. **Cyber baselines are near chance.** This makes the 93.1% open-weight Cyber ASR weak evidence: the eligible set is mostly each model's default label.
4. **Cyber has a format confound.** The clean branch has no analyst-note block, while the attacked branch adds one. Only the Luna ablation controls for "a note is present" (the random-content arm), and there the content effect is p = 0.136.
5. **The evaluation is circular.** Luna is the attacker, the support judge, the blind detector, the ablation victim and a debate member. Nobody audits "truthful" by hand.
6. **Confidence comparisons across domains are not like-for-like.** The two domains select responses differently (minimum over candidates vs first success).
7. **Reported reliance is collected but never analyzed.** Victims are asked which evidence ids they relied on (App. K), but the paper never reports whether flipped victims cite the injected sentence. That would have been a direct test of whether stated reliance tracks real influence.
8. **Proposition 1 is never tested against data.** There is no estimate of the margins γ or of the attainable strengths η, and L(w) and D(w) are heuristics.

## Assumptions the paper does not test

- **That the benchmark label is the warranted answer** (ȳ(q, E) = y\*), so every flip is an error. PubMedQA's "maybe" label and short abstracts leave room for defensible disagreement. A flip after a true caveat may sometimes be a legitimate update, and nobody adjudicates by hand.
- **That success runs through the targeted warrant.** The only evidence is behavioral contrasts. There is no check of the victims' cited ids, no probing and no attention analysis.
- **That an inserted "analyst note" or extra abstract sentence is a realistic channel** in deployed systems.
- **That single-shot results transfer to multi-step agents** that can re-read the source, run checks or query tools.

## Overlap with the idea

| Comp. | Coverage | Where | What overlaps / what does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Setting | not | Sec. 4; App. I–K | Single-turn classification. The code task is vulnerability detection on one function (at most 2500 characters): no repair, tools, loop or graph. Small open models are included (7B–32B; 8192-token context for the 32B and Phi-R models), but context pressure is never varied. |
| C2 Delivery | not | App. I, K; Table 12 | Everything is pushed into the prompt. There is no pull condition and no presentation variants. Side result: in Cyber, a neutral restatement of grounds that are already visible flips 17% (single candidate) and 28% (best-of-3), not different from ORA (p = 0.136). The salience of pushed content alone moves decisions. |
| C3 Truth | partly | Sec. 3, 4.4; Tables 5, 12, 15 | It plants one misleading, relation-targeted sentence and compares it with matched controls (random warrant, neutral content) on the same items. But the planted content is **true** (it qualifies a relation rather than adding a false edge), the outcome is a flipped verdict rather than a visit to a target, and there is no base rate for "same target under true information" or for "re-ask without injection". |
| C4 Method | partly | App. I ("changes only the introduced sentence"); App. M, R | Paired counterfactual inputs that differ by one sentence, analyzed with exact McNemar on discordant pairs. But there is no saved multi-step state, no shared seed (decoding is not uniform) and no "where next" outcome; the outcome is the final label. |
| C5 Claim | partly | Sec. 4.2; Tables 3, 14; App. C, N | One visible sentence steers the decision, and statement-level checks miss it (support judge, blind detector with Exact 6.1–8.8%). There is no queryable condition to compare against, and the "ids relied on" field goes unanalyzed. |
| C6 Cross-domain | not (analogy only) | Sec. 4.2–4.3 | No market agents. The "monitors miss it" pattern has a parallel in the audits (83–92% of successes are supported and unidentified) and in the debate (peer agreement reinforces the injected reading rather than correcting it). |

**Implications for our design**
- **Cite it as the nearest "one relation misleads" neighbor, and separate ourselves on four axes:**
  - a false structural edge, not a true qualifier;
  - the next read or edit, not the final verdict;
  - a multi-step agent with tools, not a single shot;
  - delivery (pull vs push) as an experimental factor.
- **Borrow its two controls:**
  - a "random edge" arm: a decoy aimed at an irrelevant node;
  - a "neutral restatement" arm: push true graph content with no decoy.

  This keeps a decoy effect from being confused with the salience of any pushed graph text. ORA's own Cyber result shows salience alone can carry most of the effect.
- **Add what ORA lacks:**
  - a no-intervention resample base rate per fork (same state, same seed policy);
  - per-pair exact McNemar.
- **Report the K = 1 rate**, not only best-of-K.

## What it does NOT do that the idea needs

- No agent loop, tools, repository or patch outcome. No Gemma, no 4-bit quantization, no manipulation of the context limit.
- No pull-vs-push contrast with the same information. No presentation variants (bare names vs signature plus file:line).
- No false relation, since everything is deliberately kept true. No measure of whether the agent goes to a decoy's target, and no base rate for visiting that target when the information is true.
- No saved-state forks or shared-prefix continuations. The outcome is a single label.
- No test of whether tool calls or stated reliance reflect actual use.
- No market agents.
