# Same Simulation, Different Evidence: Where Retrieved Tool Output Costs LLM Agents Accuracy

- **arXiv:** 2610.26836 (cs.AI), v1, 19 Oct 2026, CC BY 4.0. 15-page PDF.
- **Authors:** Ke Zhang and Maziar Raissi (UC Riverside); Sahchit Chundur, Jerry Peprah Owusu and Mohammad Javad Abdolhosseini Qomi (UC Irvine)
- **Version read:** v1, arXiv HTML full text (`private/lit/2026-10-27/fulltext/2610.26836.txt`) and all 15 pages of the PDF (`.../2610.26836.pdf`). Read 2026-10-27.
- **Leaned-on references, looked up** with `lit.py get`:
  - PHREEQC-MCQ-200, Zhang et al. 2026b = arXiv 2607.00436. This is the source of the items: 200 MCQs. Its abstract says "21 validated PHREEQC scenarios"; this paper says the pool has 20 setups.
  - "Is grep all you need?", Sen et al. 2026 = arXiv 2605.15184. The closest prior work. As summarised here, file-based delivery of search results lowers accuracy in 9 of 10 harness–model pairs, with no mechanism identified.
  - Xie et al. 2026 = arXiv 2610.03769. Bayes-sufficient messages can still fail bounded receivers; the loss splits into externalization, absorption and action closure. This paper's exposure and closure map onto absorption and closure.
  - Not re-read: Xu et al. 2020 (usable information) and Tan et al. 2024 (models prefer self-generated over retrieved context).

**One-line verdict:** this is the closest precedent for our delivery contrast (C2) and our fork method (C4), in a different domain.
- The same tool output is either put in the tool result (inline) or kept one read away behind a section index (TOC). Every number is reachable in both arms.
- A paired fork from a saved GPT-5.2 state varies only the pending observation: −9.4 pp [−14.5, −4.6] over 131 items × 3 (App. D).
- The loss is in what the agent reads and uses, not what it computes. Under TOC, GPT-5.1/5.2 skip the report in 53–60% of trajectories and answer from their own files.
- The effect is model-dependent: no net cost for Claude Opus/Sonnet 4.6, and GPT-5.4's null hides a loss of about 10 pp.
- But the domain is a geochemistry simulator, all information is true (no decoy), the pushed content is output the agent asked for, the models are closed frontier ones, and the forks are scored on the final MCQ answer, not on the next read.
- Coverage: C1 not, C2 partly, C3 not, C4 partly, C5 partly, C6 not.

## Claim

1. **The cost of retrieval depends on the model** (§3, Table 1). TOC minus inline, pooled over all runs per item:

   | Model | Δ (pp) | 95% CI | p_Holm |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Opus 4.6 | −0.6 | [−3.8, +2.5] | 1.000 |
   | Sonnet 4.6 | −0.5 | [−3.8, +3.2] | 1.000 |
   | GPT-5.4 | +0.2 | [−5.5, +5.7] | 1.000 |
   | GPT-5.2 | −7.9 | [−14.9, −0.9] | 0.128 (unadjusted 0.032) |
   | GPT-5.1 | −11.3 | [−18.0, −4.9] | 0.005 |

   - Keyword search (Grep) does no better than TOC.
2. **The loss lies in what agents read, not in what they compute** (§4; Tables 3, 4).
   - Build rates are similar across interfaces.
   - Under TOC, agents skip the report and answer from their own SELECTED_OUTPUT files, which print more digits. They then truncate to a neighbouring option. The authors call this "evidence substitution".
   - With the simulation held fixed, TOC still costs GPT-5.2 −9.4 pp (App. D).
3. **A null net effect can hide the loss** (§4, Table 9).
   - GPT-5.4 loses exposure −2.6 [−4.8, −0.5] and closure −7.2 [−11.1, −4.2].
   - It gains build +4.1 [+0.0, +8.1].
   - Net: −4.4 [−12.4, +3.4] on numeric items, and +0.2 pooled.
4. **Intervening on what agents read removes the closure loss** (§5, Table 5).
   - Naming the authoritative output (TOC+Report) beats all TOC runs by +7.9 (GPT-5.2), +6.3 (GPT-5.1) and +7.8 [+2.2, +13.6] pp (GPT-5.4).
   - Inlining what fits (Hybrid) works as well.
   - A rounding instruction (TOC+Round) does not: +0.6 and +1.2 pp.
5. **Harness advice** (§6): judge context management by where accuracy is lost, not only by final accuracy. "Inlining outputs that fit is a safe default"; otherwise, name the authoritative output.

## Method (enough to reproduce)

1. **Items** (§2.1, App. A, Tables 6–7).
   - The pool is the 200-item, four-option MCQ set of PHREEQC-MCQ-200, written by one domain expert. Each item asks for one quantity from a simulation, e.g. a pH at a reaction step, a saturation index, or a molality in a transport cell.
   - Every item was re-executed in PHREEQC 3.8.6 with a frozen, author-supplied phreeqc.dat:
     - The author's own input was used for 9 of 20 setups.
     - Otherwise the item text was encoded literally with defaults: mmol/kgw, 25 °C, pe 4.
     - Inputs were never adjusted toward the key.
     - Redox-sensitive items were re-run with tighter convergence and alternative step-size and integrator settings.
   - An item is kept only if its printed value matches the key exactly and uniquely, and is stable.
   - Result: 158 items from 19 setups kept, 137 of them numeric. The 42 excluded items were all confirmed defective by the item author (Table 6). Model outputs played no part in the selection.
   - Distractors usually differ from the key in the last printed digit.
   - A strict subset of 134 items drops 24 items whose setup text under-specifies the input.
2. **Agent** (§2.2; App. B, Fig. 2).
   - Four tools: `write_file`, `read_file` (with an optional line range), `list_file` and `execute_phreeqc`.
   - An isolated workspace, at most 24 steps, and one tool per turn. The answer is written to `final_answer.txt` as `<<< X >>>`.
   - System prompt:
     - a geochemist persona
     - rules: relative paths, no DATABASE line, consult `database_reference.txt`, answer from PHREEQC output, re-run if no option matches
     - one condition-specific line describing `execute_phreeqc`
     - the mode name
3. **Conditions** (App. B, "Tool results"; Fig. 3).
   - **Every condition:** each `execute_phreeqc` call returns the exit status, the last 4,000 characters of stdout/stderr, and the names and sizes of the files created.
   - **Raw100k (inline):**
     - Adds the report. Reports over 100k characters are cut to their first and last 50k, with a marker.
     - Its prompt line tells the agent to use `read_file` only for another file or to re-read a portion.
   - **TOC:**
     - Adds an index of the report's section headers with their starting lines: at most 50 entries, the first and last 25 if there are more.
     - Adds a hint to read line ranges. Its prompt line says contents are not included and must be read.
   - **Grep:** adds only the report's line count, plus a `grep_file` tool. `read_file` on a large file without a range returns nothing.
   - **Hybrid:** the report inline when it is ≤ 100k characters, otherwise the index plus the report length.
   - **TOC+Report and TOC+Round:** the TOC payload plus a three-sentence instruction, stated in the system prompt and appended to every `execute_phreeqc` result.
     - Report: values in `result.out` are authoritative and printed at the options' precision; read that section; do not answer from SELECTED_OUTPUT or USER_PUNCH files.
     - Round: round to the options' precision; do not truncate; no file is named.
   - **`read_file` without a range** returns files ≤ 20,000 characters whole, and otherwise the first and last 80 lines.
   - **A harness bug:** in the headline and rerun TOC runs, the index was empty for reports over 100k characters. On those 33 items, all four TOC runs reuse one separate run that had the index built.
4. **Models** (§2.2).
   - Claude Opus 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 4.6: temperature 0, no extended thinking.
   - GPT-5.4, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.1 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview: API defaults, with no reasoning-effort setting.
   - Direct and CoT prompting without tools serve as baselines.
5. **Runs** (Table 8):

   | Model | Raw100k | TOC | Grep | Hybrid | TOC+Report | TOC+Round |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Opus 4.6 | 1 | 1 | – | – | – | – |
   | Sonnet 4.6 | 1 | 4 | 1 | – | – | – |
   | GPT-5.4 | 2 | 4 | – | – | 2 | – |
   | GPT-5.2 | 2 | 4+1 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
   | GPT-5.1 | 2 | 4+1 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
   | Gemini 3 Flash | 1 | 1 | – | – | – | – |

   - "+1" is the TOC control run for the interventions.
   - The conditions were run at different times.
6. **Failure localisation** (§2.3, App. C).
   - 5,773 full-agent trajectories with complete logs.
   - Each `execute_phreeqc` input was rebuilt from the preceding `write_file` events and re-run offline with the same binary and database: 11,007 calls, 10,605 distinct inputs.
   - Re-running each trajectory's last execution reproduced its stored report in 4,971 of 5,009 cases; the rest differ only in line order.
   - **Produced** = every output of these runs.
   - **Seen** = every tool result carrying simulator output: inline payloads, `read_file` results on the report or on selected-output files, and search matches.
   - **Match rule:**
     - the printed number equals the option at its precision, or rounds to it when printed with more digits
     - midpoints match both neighbours
     - the sign is ignored for dissolved, precipitated or changed quantities
     - conductance options match only on conductance lines
     - the input echo is excluded
7. **Shapley attribution.** Each condition's accuracy change, relative to the same model's single logged inline run, is split into build, exposure, closure and unsupported terms.
   - 1,000 item-bootstrap resamples, with items resampled jointly for both conditions.
   - The authors state that it "allocates an observed difference … and does not estimate independent causal effects".
8. **Paired replay** (§4, App. D, Table 10). This is the fork design.
   - **Checkpoint:** the first execution with exit code 0 in GPT-5.2's logged inline run, taken before the result reaches the model.
     - 131 of 137 numeric items have one.
     - Selection uses only the exit status, not the answer or whether the output holds the key.
   - **Rebuild:** history and workspace are rebuilt by replaying the earlier file writes and PHREEQC calls in order, failed ones included. Files are verified by hash in both arms.
     - 115 reports re-match the recorded report after normalising paths, timing and separators.
     - The other 16 were capped to head and tail when recorded.
   - **The two arms differ only in the pending result:**
     - inline: the report, with the usual 100k cap
     - TOC: the section index, built at every size
   - **Shared:** one common system prompt (which one is not stated), the visible history, the file-reading tools, and the remaining 24 − s turns.
     - The agent can list and read the frozen files and submit an answer. It cannot re-run the simulator.
   - **Sampling:**
     - 3 continuations per item per arm, with GPT-5.2 API defaults and a 4,096-token completion cap per call.
     - Item order and arm order are shuffled with seed 20261011, and paired arms run back to back.
     - The model's sampling is not seeded.
     - The first repeat was inspected before the other two were run. That makes 786 continuations.
9. **Manual audit** (App. C): 68 trajectories, read independently of the automatic labels:
   - 12 TOC losses each for GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2, each read alongside the inline trajectory
   - 6 Grep losses for GPT-5.2
   - 8 Sonnet TOC failures
10. **Statistics:**
    - 95% item-bootstrap intervals.
    - Sign-flip permutation tests, Holm-corrected over the five models.
    - Sensitivity checks: resampling setups, leaving one setup out, and the 158 / 200 / 134 item sets.
    - For the replay: 50,000 paired-item bootstrap resamples (seed 20261011), plus setup resampling.

## Setting, data, models

- **Simulator:** PHREEQC 3.8.6, the USGS aqueous-geochemistry simulator. Its reports run up to 846.8k characters.
- **Items:** 158 verified MCQ items (137 numeric) from 19 setups. Majority-label rate 32.9%.
- **Models:** Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.1 and Gemini 3 Flash Preview. All are closed and called through APIs.
- **Context:** inline output is capped at 100k characters. Mean input tokens per item range from 28.3k to 100.1k (Table 2).
- **Absent:** code, graphs, edits, repair, and any small, open or quantised model.

## Metric (exact definitions)

- **Accuracy:** correct answers out of the 158 items. Unparseable or missing answers count as wrong.
- **Primary contrast:** per-item accuracy under TOC minus inline, with every run of each condition pooled per item. 95% item-bootstrap CI; sign-flip permutation p, Holm-corrected over five models.
- **Stage rates,** on the 137 numeric items:
  - **Build p:** some simulation the agent itself ran printed the keyed value.
  - **Exposure s:** given that it was produced, the value reached the agent (inline, or through a read or search result).
  - **Closure c:** given that it was seen, the agent answered correctly.
  - Accuracy = p·s·c + the accuracy of "unsupported" answers, given without the keyed value in view.
- **Evidence use** (Tables 4–5):
  - **Skip:** no part of `result.out` reached the agent.
  - **Own:** the agent read one of its own selected-output files.
  - **Def.:** the agent defined a selected-output file.
  - **Digits:** among trajectories that saw the keyed value, the share that saw it only with more digits than the report prints.
  - **Trap:** among trajectories that saw a value that rounds to the key but truncates to a distractor, the number that chose the distractor.
  - **Corr. P / Corr. D:** accuracy when the keyed value was seen as the report prints it / only with more digits.
- **Replay Δ:** per item, the mean of the 3 paired TOC − inline differences; then the mean over the 131 items. Percentile 95% CI.

## Limitations

**The authors' own** (§9; App. B):
- closed models only
- Claude at temperature 0 without thinking, and GPT at defaults, so explicit reasoning is untested
- the full-agent conditions differ in one system-prompt line, and the instruction conditions repeat their instruction in every result
- one wording per instruction
- not every model was run in every condition
- runs took place at different times, and possible model updates are not modelled
- the four TOC runs share one run on the 33 long items
- one logged inline run per model anchors the decomposition
- the replay covers only GPT-5.2, from inline checkpoints
- verification is not an independent scientific review
- 24 items are under-specified

**Mine:**
1. **The replay's system prompt is not identified.** The text says only "a common system prompt". Whichever line it uses (inline's "reads only if needed" or TOC's "contents not included"), one arm sees a tool description that does not match its payload. The paper does not say which.
2. **The replay pairs prefixes, not samples.**
   - The model's sampling is not seeded (API defaults); the seed only shuffles order.
   - The first repeat was inspected before the other two were run.
   - Only accuracy is reported for the forks. No Skip or Own rates, so the "where it reads" evidence is observational, from the unpaired full runs.
3. **The instruction changes what agents write, not only what they read.**
   - The share that defines a selected-output file drops from 98.7 to 42.1 (GPT-5.2), 81.0 to 38.0 (GPT-5.1) and 83.3 to 3.8 (GPT-5.4) (Table 5, App. B).
   - Build rates hold, but "changing only what they read" (§5) overstates how clean the intervention is.
4. **GPT-5.2's full-agent loss is weak on its own.**
   - p_Holm = 0.128, and the setup-resampled interval [−16.2, +1.0] includes zero.
   - The abstract reports "lose … 7.9 points" with no qualifier. The replay is the stronger evidence.
5. **Single runs are noisy.**
   - GPT-5.4's TOC runs span 69.0–76.6% and its inline runs 70.9 / 76.6% (Table 2).
   - GPT-5.2's TOC control (63.3%) sits above all four other TOC runs (55.7–60.1%). So its instruction gain is +3.5 against the control but +7.9 against all TOC runs.
   - The control comparisons rest on one run each.
6. **The decomposition baseline is one inline run.** For GPT-5.2 it is the higher of its two inline runs (67.7 vs 65.8); for GPT-5.1 the lower (65.8 vs 67.7). This tilts the size of the attributed loss, though not its sign.
7. **"Information-equivalent" (§6) means reachable, not in view.** Inline is cut to head and tail for 33 items (16 replay checkpoints). The authors handle this by reporting fit-only subsets.
8. **"No cost for Claude" is a net statement.** There are no full Opus logs and no Sonnet inline logs, so offsetting effects like GPT-5.4's cannot be checked. Sonnet's low Skip (10.5%) and Trap (1/35) only suggest little substitution.
9. **Labels rest on numeric matching.** Agreement with the manual stages is 52/58 on an outcome-selected sample. The authors call this indicative.
10. **Cross-paper discrepancy:** the source benchmark's abstract says 21 validated scenarios, this paper says 20 setups. It is minor.

## Assumptions the paper does not test

- That substitution reflects a poor choice of evidence rather than plain convenience. The authors themselves note that "agents may read these files simply because they are short".
- That the replay result holds for other models, or with reasoning effort turned on.
- That the competing evidence could be false. Here it is always true, only more precise.
- That the same pattern holds when the pushed content is unrequested. Here the agent always asked for the simulation.
- That it holds for relational or structural content (calls, dependencies) rather than numeric reports.
- That it holds under tight context budgets. Inline here is 100k characters.

## Push vs pull focus: what it implies for a coding agent (my inference)

- **Their contrast is our C2 at the content level.** The same content is either in view (pushed into the tool result) or one read away (pulled with `read_file`), and the same information is reachable in both. For GPT-5.1/5.2, in view beats pullable.
- **The mechanism is substitution:** the agent does not pull the canonical content and answers from evidence it already holds. In a coding agent, the analogue would be answering or editing from its own earlier grep and file reads, or from priors about the repo, instead of from a graph tool's result. That is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- **Size and power:** −9.4 pp in the replay, with an interval half-width of about 5 pp at 131 items × 3 forks. That is roughly the precision we can expect at about 106 tasks × 3 forks. It matches our power note: effects under about 5 pp will not be detectable.
- **Model dependence and masking are real.** Report per model, and decompose into queried / seen / used / acted on. A net null (GPT-5.4) can hide a loss of about 10 pp.
- **A pointer beats generic advice.** Naming the authoritative source in every tool result restored use; a generic "round correctly" did not. For us, a pushed pointer to the right node may carry behaviour where a generic "use the graph" instruction does not. This is untested for code graphs.
- **Design hazards to copy their fixes for:**
  - Keep the tool's description identical across arms.
  - In forks, fix the system prompt and say which one it is.
  - Pick checkpoints on a criterion independent of the outcome.
  - Verify the workspace by hash.
  - Shuffle arm order.
  - Seed the model's sampling as well, which they did not.
- **Under our 16k cap, inline delivery of large outputs is impossible.** Push has to be compact (bare names vs names + signature + file:line), which this paper does not test.

## What it does NOT do that the idea needs

- Code-graph structure, and pushing facts the agent did not ask for vs the agent's own query.
- A planted false relation, with the rate of following it compared against a true-information base rate on the same target.
- Scoring the next read or edit in the paired forks, across several models, with seeded sampling.
- A small open quantised model under a 16k cap, end-to-end repair, and interface-failure counts.
- Presentation variants of structure (bare names vs names + signature + file:line).

**Useful for us:**
- **The replay recipe** (App. D) is directly reusable: an outcome-independent checkpoint, a hash-verified workspace, one shared prompt, shuffled arm order, 3 continuations per arm, an item bootstrap, and setup-level resampling. Their interval width calibrates our power.
- **The stage decomposition** (build / exposure / closure / unsupported, with Shapley terms and a bootstrap) maps onto our queried / seen / used. "Seen" can be defined by matching the target's name in any observation.
- **Control the tool description.** In their full runs, the inline wording discouraged reads while the TOC wording required them. Keep the tool description identical across arms.
- **Expect model dependence and masked nulls.** Pre-register per-model analyses.
- **"Evidence substitution"** gives us language and a mechanism to test: under pull, does a coding agent act on its own earlier observations instead of the graph tool's answer?
