## Claim (key numbers and where they are)

The setup is a "matched loop": the agent loop, prompt, action space, 8-step budget and greedy decoding are the same in every condition, and only the observation the tool returns changes. Under it, persistent misleading observations make a tool-using agent worse than the same agent whose tool returns no evidence. The paper calls this a value inversion.

- **HotpotQA, Qwen2.5-7B:** 44.8 F1 Clean, 22.3 No Feedback, 4.7 Shuffled (abstract; Table 1; Figure 2).
  - Paired-bootstrap point estimates (Section 3.1): Clean minus No Feedback is 22.5 F1, and No Feedback minus Shuffled is 17.6 F1.
  - The bootstrap intervals are not printed. The McNemar p-values behind "exact paired tests on trajectory success support the same ordering" are not printed either.
- **The same ordering holds in all six cells of Table 1** (Clean / No Feedback / Shuffled):
  - HotpotQA F1: Qwen2.5-3B 42.1 / 11.4 / 0.3; Llama-3.1-8B 39.1 / 2.9 / 0.1; GPT-4o 65.8 / 52.7 / 2.8.
  - FEVER accuracy: Qwen2.5-7B 84.2 / 67.1 / 11.0; Llama-3.1-8B 68.2 / 42.5 / 3.1.
- **Stronger retrieval does not remove it.** An oracle-style clean retriever raises Clean to 54.9 F1 and leaves No Feedback (22.6) and Shuffled (4.8) unchanged (Table 2, Section 3.2).
- **Plausible wrong evidence still inverts; recoverable evidence does not** (Table 2, Section 3.3).
  - Same-question distractors (Plausible) score 9.5 F1, below the 22.3 fallback.
  - One early distractor followed by clean evidence (Conflicting) scores 39.1 F1: above fallback, below Clean.
- **Scope checks keep the ordering** (Table 3):
  - Plan-then-act: Qwen2.5-7B 47.8 / 16.5 / 0.2; Llama-3.1-8B 40.0 / 0.7 / 0.0.
  - GSM8K with a calculator, Qwen2.5-7B: 83.0 / 52.0 / 14.0 accuracy.
- **First-step features predict failure** (Table 4).
  - AUC 0.796 ± 0.036 for Qwen2.5-7B and 0.849 ± 0.023 for Llama-3.1-8B.
  - On Clean+Plausible only: 0.751 and 0.763.
  - The predictor transfers at chance to Conflicting: AUC 0.516 (Section 5.3, Appendix B.3).
- **Corrupting only the first search result** (Figure 3, Section 5.2):
  - Qwen2.5-7B success falls from 42.0% to 20.0% (2.3% under full corruption).
  - GPT-4o goes from 63.0% to 61.0% (4.0% under full corruption).
- **Hedging:** first-step hedging goes with failure, not recovery (Section 5.4; Appendix B.3, direction only).
- **Simple repairs are small or depend on the model** (Table 5):
  - Skeptical prompting: +1.4 to +5.7 F1.
  - Re-query verification: +0.5 to +5.9 on Qwen and -1.7 to -4.8 on Llama.
  - The within-model split meant to show that verification is "fallback-limited" misses its own pre-registered threshold (Table 6, Sections 6 and 9).

## C4 in detail: how the matched loop is matched

- **Re-runs from the start, not forks.** Each condition is its own full episode on the same question (Section 2, Appendix A.1). The following are identical across conditions:
  - prompt and tool interface;
  - action space (search, lookup, finish);
  - the 8-step budget;
  - decoding: temperature 0.0, at most 400 tokens per step, "deterministic seed 42", and stop sequences at the next observation field.

  No agent state is saved and restored, and nothing is forked mid-trajectory.
- **The prefix is shared only by construction.** With greedy decoding and an identical prompt, the first thought and the first action are the same in every condition. So the conditions in effect branch at the first observation.
  - The paper never says this and never checks that the prefixes match.
  - It does not discuss GPU nondeterminism.
  - Targeted-corruption runs "reuse the same seed" (Appendix A.4).
- **Seeds.** Seed 42 both picks the question sample and seeds decoding. There is one seed, with no variance across seeds or reruns.
- **Where the observation is varied:**
  - at every tool call: Shuffled, Plausible, No Feedback, and GSM8K (per expression);
  - or at the first search only: Conflicting, and the targeted first-step condition of Figure 3;
  - or, in the mitigation runs, on each call with probability 0.3, 0.5 or 0.7.

  It is never varied at a chosen later step while the rest of the trajectory is held.
- **Outcome measured.** The final answer: F1/EM, label or numeric accuracy, and a binary success flag (F1 ≥ 0.6) used for exact McNemar tests. Secondary measures:
  - first-step lexical and length features, used as predictors;
  - one mean step count (GPT-4o, 7.81 steps under Shuffled);
  - the verifier's accept counts.
- **Navigation: never measured.** The paper says misleading evidence redirects the search trajectory (Sections 2.1, 3.3 and 8), but it reports none of the following:
  - what the agent searches or looks up after a misleading observation;
  - whether later queries target entities from that observation;
  - whether the final answer is copied from it.

  The first-step query features describe the query written before any observation arrives.
- **Pairing unit:** the question. Conditions are paired over 300 (HotpotQA, open models), 292 (FEVER) or 100 (GPT-4o, GSM8K) questions.

## Method (enough to reproduce)

**Agent and environment (Section 2.1, Appendix A.1-A.2).**
- A ReAct-style loop with search, lookup and finish actions over a Wikipedia-like environment. For HotpotQA the pages are the question's own context packet: the paper calls them "question-local pages" (Section 2.3, Appendix A.3).
- Budget, decoding and stop rules as above. GPT-4o (gpt-4o-2024-08-06) runs through the chat-completions API, with the same temperature and stop rules, via a batched wrapper.
- The agent prompt is not printed.
- **Default retriever:** exact title match, then fuzzy title match (SequenceMatcher, threshold 0.3), then lexical-overlap search over titles and paragraphs.
- **Oracle-style retriever:** the same interface, with candidates restricted to gold supporting titles. If nothing matches, it returns a deterministic supporting page.

**Observation conditions (Section 2.2).**
- **Clean:** faithful evidence.
- **Corrupted:** misleading evidence through the same interface.
- **No Feedback:** the environment withholds task evidence and the agent stays inside the loop. The string returned in place of evidence is not specified.

**Corruption regimes (Section 2.3, Appendix A.3).** All are applied at the observation level.
- **Shuffled** (the default corrupted condition): every observation is replaced by off-target paragraphs from the question's context. Persistent.
- **Plausible:** search is restricted to same-question distractor titles, meaning context pages not listed in the supporting facts. Persistent.
- **Conflicting:** the first search call is forced onto a same-question distractor. The clean backend resumes after it.
- **Targeted first-step corruption** (Section 5.2, Figure 3): "corrupting only the first search result". Appendix A does not give its corruption type.
- **GSM8K calculator:** corruption is persistent per expression.
  - Integer results are shifted by about 10% (minimum offset 1).
  - Decimal results are shifted by at least 0.5.
  - The same wrong value is cached for repeated queries.
- **Mitigation runs:** per-call corruption at 30%, 50% and 70% (Table 5, Appendix C.1). The corruption type at these rates and the base F1 at each rate are not reported.

**Scope variant.** Plan-then-act: the model writes a short plan, then enters the same loop (Section 2.1).

**Repairs (Section 6, Appendix C).**
- **Skeptical prompting:** an explicit warning that tool feedback may be unreliable. The wording is not given.
- **Re-query verifier:** environment-side and not learned.
  - For each query the environment samples two observations independently and computes the Jaccard overlap of their token sets.
  - At 0.5 or above, the observation is accepted. Otherwise it is replaced with a no-results message.
  - False accepts are scored against the simulator's corruption labels, not answer correctness.

**Failure predictor (Section 5.1, Appendix B.1).**
- A standardized logistic regression, class-balanced, with default L2.
- Features observable after the first tool interaction:
  - lexical overlaps among the question, the search query, the first returned title and the first observation;
  - query length, first-thought length and observation length;
  - hedging and uncertainty markers in "the first-step text";
  - surface cues: years and punctuation density.
- Gold support titles are never used as features.
- 5-fold cross-validation grouped by question_id. AUC is computed from held-out probabilities.

**Within-model verification split (Appendix C.3, Table 6).** Qwen2.5-7B on the same 300 HotpotQA questions, split into easy and hard by four proxies:
- correctness under No Feedback (60 easy / 240 hard);
- closed-book QA correctness (73/227);
- "ensemble-strict" (72/220): easy if at least 2 of 3 closed-book prompts are right, hard if 0 of 3, with 1-of-3 questions dropped;
- ensemble-strict under oracle retrieval.

The pre-registered success criterion was p < 0.01 on both sides.

**Statistics (Section 2.4).** Exact McNemar tests on paired binary success; paired bootstrap for intervals on headline gaps.

## Setting, data, models

- **HotpotQA:** a 300-question seed-42 sample of the distractor dev split for the open models. GPT-4o uses 100 seed-42 questions.
- **FEVER:** a 292-example "filtered validation subset described in the main text" (Appendix A.1). The main text never describes the filter or the label set.
- **GSM8K:** 100 examples with a calculator tool, Qwen2.5-7B only (a pilot).
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-3B (Table 1 only), Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and GPT-4o (HotpotQA only). The paper gives no quantization, precision or context-length details.

## Metrics (exact definitions, Section 2.4)

- **HotpotQA:** exact match and token-level F1. **FEVER:** label accuracy. **GSM8K:** exact numeric accuracy.
- **Trajectory success**, used only for trajectory analyses and paired tests: normalized answer-overlap F1 ≥ 0.6.
- **Value inversion:** Corrupted scores below No Feedback under the matched loop (Section 2.2). The Clean-to-No-Feedback gap measures what reliable evidence adds; the No-Feedback-to-Corrupted gap tests inversion.
- **Failure prediction:** held-out, threshold-free AUC.
- **Verifier:** accepted / total observations; false accepts against the simulator's labels.

## Results (including weak and negative ones)

- **Core ordering:** Clean > No Feedback > Shuffled in all six model-task cells (Table 1).
- **Retrieval strength** (Table 2), F1 with EM in parentheses:
  - oracle: Clean 54.9 (42.0), No Feedback 22.6 (16.3), Shuffled 4.8 (2.3);
  - heuristic: Clean 44.8 (33.7), No Feedback 22.3 (15.3), Shuffled 4.7 (2.0).
- **Regimes** (Table 2): Plausible 9.5 F1 (EM 6.7); Conflicting 39.1 F1 (EM 29.7).
- **Step count:** GPT-4o under Shuffled takes 7.81 steps on average against the 8-step cap (Appendix A.4). The paper reads this as floundering rather than parser failure.
- **Predictor:**
  - Per-fold AUCs for Qwen2.5-7B: 0.835, 0.774, 0.737, 0.785, 0.850 (Appendix B.2).
  - On Conflicting, the transferred predictor has AUC 0.516. At threshold 0.5 it predicts 71.7% failure against an actual 62.3% (Appendix B.3).
- **Feature trends** (Appendix B.3): higher title-query and title-question overlap goes with success; longer first thoughts, longer first queries and more hedging go with failure.
- **Verifier** (Appendix C.1): it accepts 527/1043 observations at 30% corruption, 257/1106 at 50% and 106/1279 at 70%, with zero measured false accepts.
- **Mitigation deltas** (Table 5), F1 at 30 / 50 / 70% corruption:
  - skeptical prompt: Qwen +4.9 / +2.9 / +5.7; Llama +3.2 / +4.6 / +1.4;
  - verification: Qwen +0.5 / +3.6 / +5.9; Llama -1.7 / -3.3 / -4.8.
- **Within-model split** (Table 6):
  - Easy-side gains reach significance at 50% and 70% for the three non-oracle proxies. For example, the No Feedback proxy gives +26.7 (p = 0.004) and +50.0 (p < 0.001); closed-book gives +27.4 at 70% (p < 0.001).
  - Hard-side deltas are negative but not significant, except -8.2 at 50% under oracle + ensemble-strict (p = 0.030).
  - The pre-registered p < 0.01 on both sides is never met. The paper reports the result as "directional" (Sections 6 and 9).
- **Weak and negative results the paper reports itself:**
  - Conflicting recovers.
  - The first-step predictor is at chance on Conflicting.
  - Verification hurts Llama.
  - Skeptical prompting gains little.

## Limitations: the authors' (Section 9)

- GPT-4o is the only proprietary model, and it runs on HotpotQA only.
- The calculator experiment is a pilot.
- The verification analysis is directional: the pre-registered threshold is not consistently met.
- Retrieval strength compares a lightweight default with an oracle-style backend. There is no BM25 or dense retriever in between.

## Limitations: mine

1. **The inversion is never split into wrong answers and non-answers.**
   - Under Shuffled, GPT-4o uses 7.81 of 8 steps on average (Appendix A.4).
   - FEVER accuracy under Shuffled is 11.0% (Qwen2.5-7B) and 3.1% (Llama-3.1-8B). That is below uniform guessing over FEVER labels (33% for three labels, 50% for two).
   - Off-topic paragraphs cannot argue for the opposite verdict. So much of this loss is likely missing labels, step-cap exhaustion or NOT ENOUGH INFO answers.
   - The paper reports no abstention, no-answer or step-cap rates by condition.
   - So "worse than fallback" may largely mean that useless evidence keeps the agent searching instead of answering from memory. That is a stopping-policy effect, not adoption of misleading content. This is my inference; the paper does not break errors down.
2. **Following is asserted, never measured.** No result shows the agent adopting what the misleading observation says: not as an answer, and not as a next query.
3. **Hedging is probably measured before the evidence arrives.**
   - The hedging and first-thought-length features come from "the first-step text".
   - In a ReAct step, the first thought is written before the first observation. At temperature 0 it is identical across Clean, Shuffled and Plausible for a given question.
   - If those are the features, they measure question difficulty, not a reaction to misleading evidence. Section 5.4's reading, that the agent sounds doubtful while still following misleading evidence, would then not follow from them.
   - The paper does not say which text the hedging count covers. The claim that models grow more verbose and uncertain under harmful conditions (Section 5.4) has no number behind it.
4. **Two first-step manipulations disagree, and the paper does not reconcile them.**
   - Conflicting (first search forced onto a same-question distractor, then clean) leaves Qwen2.5-7B success at about 37.7%. That is my arithmetic from the 62.3% actual failure rate in Appendix B.3, against 42.0% Clean.
   - "First-step corrupted" in Figure 3 leaves 20.0%.
   - Both corrupt only the first search. One is used for "concentrated anchoring" and the other for "early bad evidence is not destiny".
   - The targeted condition's corruption type is not defined.
5. **The verifier's numbers fit "accept only when both samples are clean".**
   - Its acceptance rates are 0.505, 0.232 and 0.083 at corruption rates 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7.
   - If each of the two samples is independently corrupted with probability p, and only clean pairs pass, acceptance would be (1-p)² = 0.49, 0.25 and 0.09. This matches (my arithmetic).
   - If so, three things follow:
     - The verifier also discards a share p of clean observations.
     - Its zero false accepts come from corrupted samples being random paragraphs that rarely overlap.
     - At 70% corruption the agent sees evidence on only 8.3% of calls, so it runs mostly in No Feedback mode.
   - "Repair is limited by the fallback" is therefore confounded with "the verifier throws away most clean evidence".
   - A consistent wrong source, like the cached calculator corruption, would pass this verifier.
   - Tool calls also rise with corruption: 1043, 1106 and 1279 observations.
6. **Floor effects.**
   - No Feedback is 2.9 F1 for Llama-3.1-8B on HotpotQA and 0.7 under plan-then-act; it is 11.4 for Qwen2.5-3B. Near a zero fallback, "below fallback" is almost automatic.
   - The informative cells are Qwen2.5-7B (22.3 vs 4.7), GPT-4o (52.7 vs 2.8, n = 100), the two FEVER rows, and GSM8K (52.0 vs 14.0, n = 100).
7. **The failure predictor partly detects the condition.**
   - Its features include the overlap between the question and the first observation, which differs by condition by construction.
   - The Clean+Plausible AUC still pools two conditions, and no within-condition AUC is reported.
8. **The No Feedback proxy is close to circular.**
   - It defines "easy" as questions answered correctly with no evidence. It then shows that a verifier which mostly turns runs into No Feedback runs helps those questions (+50.0 at 70%).
   - The closed-book proxies are cleaner, and their gains are smaller (+27.4 and +27.8 at 70%).
9. **Under-specified pieces:**
   - the FEVER filter and label set;
   - the No Feedback string;
   - the targeted corruption type;
   - the 30/50/70% regime and its base scores;
   - the skeptical-prompt wording and the agent prompt.

   There is no code.
10. **Small, single-seed samples.**
    - 300, 100 and 100 questions, one seed, and no run-to-run variance.
    - GPT-4o's first-step drop is 2 questions of 100.

## Assumptions the paper does not test

- That low scores under corruption come from adopting the misleading content rather than from abstaining or running out of steps.
- That greedy re-runs reproduce the same prefix up to the intervention. This is not checked.
- That the form or placement of the evidence does not matter. Evidence is always pulled by a call and always returned in one format; nothing is ever pushed into the prompt.
- That the in-loop No Feedback arm equals the agent's parametric fallback. The agent may keep calling the tool.
- That wrong outputs from real tools vary from call to call. The verifier depends on it.
- That the results hold:
  - beyond 8 steps and 400 tokens per step;
  - under sampled decoding;
  - for other model families.

## What it does NOT do that the idea needs

- **No software engineering.** No repository, code graph, edits or tests, and no Gemma 4, 4-bit or 16k-context regime.
- **No push condition and no presentation variable.** The tool output is always pulled and always formatted the same way.
- **No planted decoy target and no base rate.** The wrong evidence is off-topic or same-question distractor pages. Nothing is planted with a known destination, and following is never scored against how often the agent goes there anyway.
- **No forks from a saved state, no short continuations, no next-step measure.** It never records where the agent reads or searches after the manipulated observation.
- **No separation of interface failures from model failures.** Step-cap exhaustion, missing labels and abstentions are not counted separately, which the project's own rules require.

What it gives the idea:
- **A recent, citable result:** wrong tool feedback can be worse than none for a small open model (Qwen2.5-7B: 4.7 vs 22.3 F1). It also gives the in-loop no-evidence arm as the right shape for a "graph withheld" control.
- **The regime a planted decoy edge sits in.** One bad first result halves Qwen2.5-7B's successes (Figure 3), while a distractor followed by clean evidence mostly recovers (Conflicting, 39.1 vs 44.8 F1). Forks from a saved state can tell which of the two a decoy edge triggers, and can measure it at the next step instead of at the final answer.
- **Its gaps are the idea's measurements:** following vs abstaining, next-step navigation, and a base rate.
